## 1. Objective

Allow a Diligent Records user to **scale their subscription** according to the
size of their business, unlocking advanced functionality (multi-user, advanced
reports, unlimited exports, priority support) in exchange for a periodic payment
in SLEARN or USDT.

The goal is twofold:

1. **Sustainability:** generate recurring revenue that funds the development
   and maintenance of Diligent Records.
2. **Fairness:** larger businesses (with greater ability to pay) contribute more,
   while small entrepreneurs keep using the tool for free.

## 2. Plan model (tentative; prices and features to be adjusted)

| Plan | Monthly price | Limits | Features |
|------|---------------|--------|----------|
| **Free** | $0 | 1 business, 100 transactions/month, 1 user, basic CSV export | All basic accounting, plan and budget functionality |
| **Small** | $2/month (or 50 SLEARN) | 3 businesses, 1,000 transactions/month, 2 users, CSV+PDF export | Advanced reports, budget vs. real, alerts |
| **Medium** | $5/month (or 120 SLEARN) | 10 businesses, 10,000 transactions/month, 5 users, API | All of the above + API, custom dashboards |
| **Large** | $15/month (or 350 SLEARN) | Unlimited businesses, unlimited transactions, 20 users, priority API | All of the above + priority support, custom reports |

**Notes:**
- Prices are tentative and will be adjusted according to the market.
- Payment can be made in SLEARN (with a discount) or in USDT.
- Entrepreneurs in Sierra Leone who completed the SBD course (R-#257) get the
  **Small plan free for 6 months** as an incentive.

## 3. Using Diligent Records for the ecosystem's own accounting (dogfooding)

A distinctive feature of this requirement is that **the same Diligent Records
infrastructure will be used to keep the books of the ecosystem itself**. This
serves two purposes at once: it is a real-world test of the tool, and it is the
actual accounting tool the operator needs for his missionary work.

Concretely, the operator (vtamara) will use Diligent Records to keep the
accounting of:

1. **The learn.tg platform itself** — income from courses, expenses for
   infrastructure, donations received, SLEARN movements, etc.
2. **Diligent Records' own operations** — development costs, hosting, marketing,
   support, etc.
3. **Missionary trips to Sierra Leone** — travel expenses (lodging, food,
   transport), donations received (FSL, churches, individuals), ministry
   expenses (water project, orphanage, crusade), with receipt photos and donor
   reports.

This use of the tool as a real book of accounts accomplishes several things:

- It exercises the tool daily, exposing bugs and missing features before they
  reach external users.
- It validates the trip mode against a real, already-existing spreadsheet
  (the operator's second missionary trip to Sierra Leone, September 2025).
- It produces donor reports directly from the tool, replacing the manual
  spreadsheet workflow.
- It demonstrates the same double-entry, multi-plan, budget-vs-real model that
  the SBD course teaches.

**Implication for the data model:** the tool must support **multiple plans
under the same user**, each with its own accounts, budgets, and transactions,
and must be able to distinguish between:

- Business plans (learn.tg, Diligent Records, etc.)
- Trip plans (missionary trips)
- Personal or ministry plans (if needed)

This is already anticipated in R-#258 §3.1 (`diligent_plan.tipo`), but this
requirement makes the *practical use* explicit: the operator is not a
hypothetical user, he is the first real user of the tool, and his own books are
the first real test.

**Implication for the subscription model:** the operator's own usage
(learn.tg + Diligent Records + trips) will likely fall in the **Medium** or
**Large** plan by volume, which gives a concrete benchmark for the plan limits.
The operator does not pay himself, but the usage data informs the design of the
plans offered to external users.

### 4.3 How this example informs the subscription design

This business plan is not a hypothetical document: it is the actual operating
plan of Diligent Records, and it will be created inside the tool as a real
`diligent_plan` with `tipo = 'business'`. From it, the subscription design
derives several concrete benchmarks:

1. **Break-even at $800/month in expenses** (development, hosting, marketing,
   trips), which means the subscription revenue target for year 1 is
   approximately **$500/month** in subscriptions plus **$300/month** in
   donations.
2. **The operator's own usage** (learn.tg + Diligent Records + missionary trips)
   will likely fall in the **Medium** or **Large** plan by volume, giving a
   concrete benchmark for what "10 businesses, 10,000 transactions/month" means
   in practice.
3. **The Small plan is the entry point** for the missionaries and pastors who
   complete the SBD course; the Free plan remains for the entrepreneurs who
   cannot pay.
4. **The pricing in SLEARN** (50, 120, 350 SLEARN/month) is calibrated so that
   an entrepreneur who completes a few courses can pay for a Small subscription
   without spending real money, reinforcing the circular economy of SLEARN.

- timestamps

## 6. Design

1. **Limit calculation.** A daily job computes `diligent_usage` for each user
   according to their plan. If a user exceeds a limit, they are notified and
   invited to upgrade (or the exceeded functionality is blocked, according to
   the policy that is defined).

2. **Payment with SLEARN.** The user can pay their subscription with SLEARN
   (earned by learning, donating, or selling in `stable-sl`). The payment is
   processed as an internal transaction: SLEARN are debited from their balance
   and recorded in `diligent_subscription_payment`.

3. **Payment with USDT.** The user can pay with USDT (on-chain, via wallet).
   The payment is verified on-chain and recorded in
   `diligent_subscription_payment`.

4. **Integration with SLEARN (R-#129).** Payment with SLEARN uses the
   `LearnTGVaultsV5` infrastructure and the SLEARN contract. No new payment
   system is created; the existing one is reused.

5. **Notifications.** The user receives:
   - A reminder 7 days before expiration.
   - A notice if they exceed a limit of their plan.
   - A suggestion to upgrade if their usage justifies it.

6. **Grace period.** If a payment fails, the user has 7 days of grace before
   their plan is downgraded to Free.

7. **Cancellation.** The user can cancel at any time. Their plan remains active
   until the end of the paid period, then downgrades to Free.

8. **Dogfooding (own accounting).** The operator's own usage (learn.tg,
   Diligent Records, missionary trips) is kept in the same infrastructure,
   under the Medium or Large plan by volume. This usage is exempt from payment
   but is used as a benchmark for the design of external plans. The business
   plan of Diligent Records itself (§4) is the first real `diligent_plan`
   created in the tool.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A user can see their current plan and usage on a settings page.
- [ ] A user can upgrade their plan (Free → Small → Medium → Large) paying with
      SLEARN or USDT.
- [ ] A user can downgrade their plan; the change applies at the end of the
      period.
- [ ] A user can cancel their subscription.
- [ ] The limits of each plan are enforced (block or notice) when exceeded.
- [ ] Payment history is recorded in `diligent_subscription_payment`.
- [ ] Usage is computed daily in `diligent_usage`.
- [ ] Payment with SLEARN debits the user's balance and records the transaction.
- [ ] Payment with USDT is verified on-chain and recorded.
- [ ] Notifications are sent at the defined moments.
- [ ] The operator can keep the books of learn.tg, Diligent Records and his
      missionary trips in the same infrastructure, under the Medium or Large
      plan by volume.
- [ ] The business plan of Diligent Records itself (§4) is created as a real
      `diligent_plan` with `tipo = 'business'`, and its budget as a real
      `diligent_budget`.
- [ ] `GET /api/diligent/subscription*` and `POST /api/diligent/subscription*`
      authenticate; `bin/audit-api-auth.mjs` reports 0 failed.
- [ ] Unit tests for the limit calculation, payment with SLEARN and the grace
      period.
- [ ] `make type` and the full suite green.

## 8. Out of scope

- Invoicing with legal validity, taxes, or integration with traditional payment
  gateways.
- Multi-user with advanced roles and permissions (to be done in a later
  requirement if the Medium/Large plan requires it).
- SLEARN/SBT integration beyond the payment of the subscription.
- The business logic of `stable-sl` (that responsibility is in another session).
