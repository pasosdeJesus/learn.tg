## Phase 1 — Church directory + minimal reputation (priority)

**Goal:** a public church directory that shows verified churches, their activity
and their reputation, with the minimum data needed to make the directory useful.
This phase generates visible value (the directory) without waiting for the full
reputation system.

### 1. Church directory

A public listing of churches that pdJ has verified. Each entry shows the church
profile, its **activity** and its **reputation**. The directory is the public
surface of the reputation system.

**Activity and reputation are separate concepts:**

| Concept | What it measures | Source | Use |
|---------|------------------|--------|-----|
| **Activity** | How much the church uses the platform | `activity_score` (the church's members' R-#278 components, §4.1), `amount_member` | Directory (show), ranking (order) |
| **Reputation** | How trustworthy the church is | pdJ verification, verified evidence, appeals | Directory (show), listing decisions |

A church can be **highly active with bad reputation** (e.g. a pastor with verified
evidence of dishonesty whose members keep completing guides) or **low activity
with good reputation** (e.g. a small verified church with no activity yet). The
directory shows both, never one instead of the other.

#### 1.1 Fields shown per region

A church whose country is region type 2 is **not published at all** (see the
header): no directory entry, no ranking row, no negative-reputation display. The
table below only contrasts the region type 1 fields with that rule.

| Field | Region type 1 | Region type 2 |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| Church name | shown | not published |
| City, country | shown | not published |
| Denomination | shown | not published |
| Lead pastor (link to public profile) | shown | not published |
| Reputation (label) | shown | not published |
| `activity_score` (aggregated) | shown (only if reputation ≥ 0) | not published |
| `amount_member` (aggregated) | shown (only if reputation ≥ 0) | not published |

`amount_member` is `COUNT(*)` of `usuario` rows with `church_id = church.id`
(lead pastor, co-pastors, leaders, members). It is an aggregate per church,
never a per-person list. Every one of those users was verified (header,
"Membership"), so the count cannot be inflated with self-created wallets.

`activity_score` is the church-level aggregation of the same components that
`platform_score` uses (§4). It measures activity, not trustworthiness.

Churches with active negative evidence do not publish `activity_score` nor
`amount_member`. Before any public signal about a negative case, the neutral
process of §2.4 applies.

The **reputation** is shown as a **label**, not a number (a score of 10 or 30 says
little): **Good** (verified church with a verified lead pastor), **Good (pastor not
registered)** (verified church without one) and **Not recommended** for a negative
case. The numeric score stays in the admin.

#### 1.2 Listing states

A negative case never starts with a public label: it runs through the neutral
process of §2.4 first. Only after that process (private claim, right of reply,
second-verifier review, strong documented evidence) can any public display exist,
and even then it is descriptive and non-accusatory ("not recommended: unresolved
dispute"), never an allegation of a crime.

| State | When | Effect |
|-------|------|--------|
| **Listed** | Church verified, no active evidence | Shown with positive reputation |
| **Under review** | An active negative case is being processed (§2.4) | Removed from the directory while the process is open; `activity_score`/`amount_member` hidden. Neither the church nor the accused is publicly accused during the process |
| **Not listed** | (a) not yet verified, (b) region type 2 (**any**), (c) third-party evidence not verifiable by pdJ | Excluded from the directory |
| **Not recommended** | A negative case completed §2.4 with strong, unanswered evidence | Shown **at the end of the directory** (in its own section, without a position number), with a neutral, descriptive notice (no allegation), `activity_score`/`amount_member` hidden, with a path to restoration (§2.4) |

#### 1.3 Who maintains the directory

- **pdJ (verifiers):** create, verify, list, unlist churches; record evidence; apply reputation.
- **Pastors:** claim their church (`/api/directory/churches/[id]/claim`) and request
  corrections. There is no region type 2 opt-in: those churches are never published
  (header), so there is nothing to opt into.
- **Menu visibility:** the ☰ shows the *Church Directory* link only to a signed-in
  user who is **verified** and lives in a **region type 1** country; guests, users
  without a country and region type 2 users do not see it.

### 2. Minimal reputation (Phase 1)

Reputation is built **only** from verification, verified evidence and appeals.
Activity does **not** grant reputation points.

#### 2.1 Church reputation (positive, minimal)

| Source | Points | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Church registered (verified by pdJ) | +10 | One time |
| Lead pastor verified | +20 | One time |

The directory shows this as the label of §1.1, never as a number.

#### 2.2 Pastor reputation (positive, minimal)

| Source | Points | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Pastor verified | +10 | One time |

#### 2.3 Negative reputation (Phase 1, internal)

Negative reputation is applied when pdJ records verified evidence against a
pastor or co-pastor. The evidence is the source of truth; the church reputation
is derived from it.

| Cause | Automatic | Effect on the church (internal) |
|-------|-----------|---------------------------------|
| Dishonesty | yes | Church reputation capped at -50 |
| Sexual abuse | yes | Church reputation capped at -50 |
| Zionism | no | Recorded, no automatic effect on the church (Phase 1) |
| Other | no | Extensible (Phase 2) |

Rules:

- **Lead pastor or co-pastor** with active evidence of `dishonesty` or
  `sexual_abuse` → church capped at -50.
- **Multiple active evidences against the same pastor** → the penalty is applied
  **once** (still -50, not accumulative).
- **Leader** with active evidence → church penalized -20 (no cap).
- **Member** with active evidence → church penalized -10 (no cap).
- **Zionism** → recorded with file, but the church is **not** penalized
  automatically in Phase 1.
- At least one file must be attached to each evidence record.
- While the case is open the church is left **out of the directory** ("under
  review"); it only returns to a public listing after §2.4, and never with a
  negative-reputation label.
- `activity_score` and `amount_member` are hidden while the negative reputation
  is active.
- **No compensation**: while the evidence is active, the church cannot show a
  positive reputation, no matter how many points its members accumulate.
- **Internal vs published**: the score above drives the platform's own decisions
  (e.g. fund access). **What is published** is governed by §2.4 — a public label
  never precedes the process.

#### 2.4 Process for negative cases (privacy and legal safety)

Publishing that a named pastor or church is "dishonest" is a serious,
defamation-prone statement the platform must be able to defend. A negative case
is handled in graduated, private-first steps (Matthew 18:15-17: first in private,
then with witnesses, then to the community):

1. **Internal flag (private).** The evidence is recorded and visible only to
   verifiers (`reputationevidence` + files); it changes nothing published.
2. **Private claim with right of reply.** The accused pastor is notified and has a
   term to answer. Silence or a refusal to answer is recorded, never taken as
   proof of guilt.
3. **Second-verifier review.** A verifier other than the one who recorded the
   evidence decides — nobody is judge in their own cause.
4. **Neutral action first (§1.2).** While the case is open the church is left
   *under review* (out of the directory); a **private** notice may go to the
   verified pastor network so those who could be harmed are warned without a
   public accusation.
5. **Public display only with strong, unanswered evidence.** The evidence must be
   strong **on its own** (documented, verifiable); the absence of a reply is
   recorded but is **not** what makes it strong. If the evidence is documented and
   unanswered, the church **is published** in the "not recommended" state — a
   **neutral, descriptive** notice ("not recommended: unresolved dispute"), never
   "thief", "abuse" or any crime label — shown **at the end of the directory, in
   its own section, without a position number**. `activity_score` and
   `amount_member` stay hidden.
6. **Restoration.** On a satisfactory reply or repair the church returns to
   *listed* with its own score. Phase 1 keeps the case internally
   (`reputationevidence` + files); the audit trail `reputationhistory` is Phase 2
   (§14.1).

**Out of scope in Phase 1:** a full appeal workflow (Phase 2, §11.6), automatic
publication, and publishing criminal allegations.

### 3. Database schema (Phase 1)

#### 3.1 `pastorreputation`

```sql
CREATE TABLE pastorreputation (
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    usuario_id INTEGER REFERENCES usuario(id) NOT NULL,
    reputation_score INTEGER DEFAULT 0,
    updated_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

#### 3.2 `reputationevidence`

```sql
CREATE TABLE reputationevidence (
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    pastorreputation_id INTEGER REFERENCES pastorreputation(id) NOT NULL,
    evidence_reason VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,   -- 'dishonesty', 'sexual_abuse', 'zionism', 'other'
    evidence_notes TEXT,                     -- private, pdJ only
    recorded_by INTEGER REFERENCES usuario(id) NOT NULL,
    recorded_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    resolved_at TIMESTAMP,                   -- NULL = active
    resolved_by INTEGER REFERENCES usuario(id),
    resolution_notes TEXT
);
```

#### 3.3 `reputationevidencefile`

```sql
CREATE TABLE reputationevidencefile (
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    reputationevidence_id INTEGER REFERENCES reputationevidence(id) NOT NULL,
    file_path VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    file_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    file_mime VARCHAR(100),
    file_size INTEGER,
    uploaded_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

At least one file per evidence record (enforced by the API and by a DB check on
insert).

#### 3.4 `churchreputation`

```sql
CREATE TABLE churchreputation (
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    church_id INTEGER REFERENCES church(id) NOT NULL,
    reputation_score INTEGER DEFAULT 0,
    updated_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

`churchreputation` does **not** store the reason. The reason lives in
`reputationevidence`; the score is derived.

#### 3.5 `church` (existing) — added columns

```sql
ALTER TABLE church ADD COLUMN is_listed BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE;
ALTER TABLE church ADD COLUMN listing_reason VARCHAR(50);
ALTER TABLE church ADD COLUMN consent_public BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE;
```

`consent_public` is a general opt-out for a church that prefers not to be listed;
it is **not** the region type 2 mechanism — those churches are never published
regardless of consent (header).

#### 3.6 `msip_pais.tipo_region` (existing)

The classification lives in `msip_pais.tipo_region` (1 = region type 1,
2 = region type 2, `NULL` = unclassified). The directory uses it to **exclude**
region type 2 churches entirely (they are not published). No new table.

#### 3.7 Trigger: recalculation

`AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE ON reputationevidence` → recalculate
`churchreputation.reputation_score` for the church of the affected pastor.

- The trigger recalculates **only the church of the affected pastor**.
- It applies the cap at -50 if there is at least one active evidence of
  `dishonesty` or `sexual_abuse` against the lead pastor or a co-pastor.
- It applies -20 if the active evidence is against a leader (no cap).
- It applies -10 if the active evidence is against a member (no cap).
- The penalty is applied **once** per role, even if the same person has multiple
  active evidences.
- `zionism` is recorded but does not change the score (Phase 1).

#### 3.8 `churchactivitycache` (table of raw sums)

`activity_score` aggregates the R-#278 components of every member of a church. The
**per-church raw sums** are kept in a table (`churchactivitycache`) refreshed by
**triggers** on the tables that feed the R-#278 components, following the cache
policy of `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§Cache Update Triggers): derived data is refreshed on
data change, not on a clock, and application code never writes the cache table.
The sums are **local**: a change in one member only touches that church's row.

The **normalization** (min-max over the directory population, §4.1) is **not
stored**: it is computed at read time from the sums, like R-#278, which computes
its windows per query instead of storing a normalized value. This keeps the cache
incremental — with a global min-max, one member's change would otherwise have to
recompute every church's `activity_score`, and changing a church's listing or
reputation would invalidate all the others.

```sql
CREATE TABLE churchactivitycache (
    church_id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES church(id),
    guide_score_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    referral_count_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    donations_usdt_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    sbt_count_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    slearn_balance_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    profilescore_sum NUMERIC NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    amount_member INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    updated_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

A table is used instead of a materialized view because PostgreSQL does not allow
triggers on `REFRESH MATERIALIZED VIEW`, and the project already uses cached
tables refreshed by triggers (`clusterscorecache` in
https://github.com/pasosdeJesus/learn.tg/issues/154 §8.2).

### 4. Activity score (Phase 1: show; Phase 2: rank)

#### 4.1 Definition

Activity is broad: answering guides, donating, referring people, holding SBTs,
keeping a SLEARN balance and completing the profile. That is exactly what
`platform_score` (R-#278 §4) captures with six weighted components:

| Component | Weight (R-#278) |
|-----------|-----------------|
| `guide_score` | 35% |
| `referral_count` | 15% |
| `donations_usdt` | 15% |
| `sbt_count` | 15% |
| `slearn_balance` | 10% |
| `profilescore` | 10% |

`activity_score` **reuses that scheme at the church level**: for each church it
sums the **raw** component values of its members, normalizes each component with
the same min-max-over-`ln(1+x)` rule over the **directory population** (region type
1, listed churches with reputation ≥ 0) — computed at read time from the per-church
sums (§3.8), once over the whole population, **never over the visitor's filtered
view**, so a church's score does not change with the country/denomination filter
(like R-#278) — and applies the same weights. It measures activity, not
trustworthiness.

**Why aggregate the raw components and normalize once at the church level**
instead of summing each member's `platform_score`: `platform_score` is a 0-100
value already normalized over the *leaderboard population*, so adding it across
members makes a church's score move when unrelated users change, and normalizing
that sum again would normalize twice. Aggregating the raw values keeps the R-#278
components and weights (the reuse the operator wants) while producing a stable,
church-relative score.

#### 4.2 Phase 1: show and order

The directory shows `activity_score` as an informational column (only for
churches with reputation ≥ 0). The list is ordered by **reputation, then
activity, then members** (descending), with the church id ascending as the final
tie-breaker; the "not recommended" section stays separate (ordered by name). The
column headers are **clickable** (`?sort=reputation|activity|members|church&order=asc|desc`)
to reorder; a chosen column becomes the primary key and the rest follow the same
chain.

#### 4.3 Phase 2: rank

The ranking uses the church-level `activity_score` (§4.1) as its metric, with the
church's reputation as a **filter**:

```
church_score = activity_score
```

- **Reputation is a filter**, not a summand:
  - Churches with `reputation_score < 0` do **not** compete in the ranking
    (they appear at the end, with the neutral notice — see §10.2).
  - Churches with `reputation_score ≥ 0` compete by `church_score`.
- **Breadth option (the operator decides).** When the goal is to favour many small
  contributors over one big one, apply the quadratic aggregation `(Σ √c_member)²`
  to the **count-like** raw components (`guide_score`, `referral_count`,
  `sbt_count`) **before** the church-level normalization. It is **not** applied to
  `donations_usdt`, `slearn_balance` or `profilescore` (amounts/scores, not counts),
  nor to the already-normalized `activity_score` — that would normalize twice.
  The quadratic is applied **per member**, so many members each with a small
  contribution outweigh one member with a large one.
- **No member cap** initially.
- **No Sybil concern**: membership is set by verifiers on verified users (header),
  so a pastor cannot create members to inflate the score — unlike Gitcoin's COCM,
  no anti-Sybil layer is needed.

|--------|--------|-----------|

| Lead pastor verified | +20 | One time |
| Co-pastor verified | +5 each | One time |
| Report submitted to pdJ (reviewed) | +5 | Per report |
| AEE participation (verified by pdJ) | +20 | Per event |

#### 9.2 Pastor reputation

| Source | Points | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Pastor verified | +10 | One time |

| Report submitted to pdJ (reviewed) | +5 | Per report |
| AEE participation (verified by pdJ) | +20 | Per event |

> **Note:** referrals, donations and course completion are **activity**, not
> reputation. They are captured by `platform_score` (R-#278 §4) and shown in the
> directory as `activity_score`. They do **not** add reputation points.

#### 9.3 Inactivity penalty

Inactivity is **not** a reputation penalty (reputation is about trust, not
activity). Inactivity is shown as activity = 0; it does not reduce reputation.

### 10. Church ranking (Phase 2)

#### 10.1 Formula

`church_score` is the church-level `activity_score`: the six R-#278 components
aggregated over the church's members and normalized **once** over the directory
population (see §4.1), with the optional breadth term of §4.3. Reputation is a
**filter**: churches with `reputation_score < 0` do not compete. No member cap
initially. No Sybil concern: membership is set by verifiers on verified users
(header).

#### 10.2 Visibility

- **Region type 1 only.** Churches in region type 2 are not listed in the ranking
  and have no directory entry (header).
- **Reputation ≥ 0 required** to compete. Churches with negative reputation
  (after §2.4) are shown **in a separate section at the end of the ranking**,
  without a position number, with the neutral notice ("not recommended"),
  `activity_score` and `amount_member` hidden.
- **Global ranking**, with a **country filter** (analogous to the GD student
  ranking, https://github.com/pasosdeJesus/learn.tg/issues/226).

#### 10.3 Columns

| Column | Source |
|--------|--------|
| Position | `ROW_NUMBER()` over `church_score DESC, church_id ASC` |
| Church | `church.name` |
| Country | `msip_pais` (region type 1 only) |
| Reputation | `churchreputation.reputation_score` (tier + points) |
| `activity_score` | church-level, §4.1 |
| `amount_member` | `COUNT(*)` of members |
| `church_score` | §10.1 |

The score below is **internal**: it drives the platform's own decisions (e.g.
fund access). **What is published** is governed by §2.4: a church whose negative
case completed both filters (private claim with right of reply and
second-verifier review) is published with the **neutral notice** ("not
recommended"), never with a negative number or a crime label. A church cannot
show a positive reputation while the evidence is active, no matter how many
points its members accumulate.

```
Internal church reputation =
  if lead pastor OR any co-pastor has active evidence of dishonesty/abuse:
    min(calculated_score, -50)
  else: calculated_score
```

#### 11.4 Effects

| Reputation level | Effect on pastor | Effect on church |
|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| -100 | Cannot register on learn.tg; cannot be pastor of any church | Church capped at -50 |
| -50 | — | No church fund access; not featured in the ranking (published with the neutral notice) |

#### 11.5 Change of pastor

| Scenario | Effect on church |
|----------|------------------|
| Lead pastor or co-pastor with active evidence | Church reputation capped at -50 (not compensable) |
| Evidence resolved | The cap is lifted; the church keeps its own score |
| New lead pastor assumes | Church keeps its own score (does not adopt the new pastor's) |
| Church becomes orphaned | Church keeps its own score (does not reset to 0) |

#### 11.6 Appeal process

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| 1. Request | Affected pastor submits appeal |
| 2. Evidence | Pastor presents evidence of change or accountability |
| 3. Review | A verifier other than the one who applied the penalty reviews the case (nobody is judge in their own cause, §2.4) |
| 4. Decision | pdJ decides to restore reputation (to 0 or positive) |

### 15. Algorithm (Phase 2)

#### 15.1 Church reputation calculation

Aggregated from §9.1, capped by active evidence (§11.3). Activity is **not**
part of the reputation.

#### 15.2 Pastor reputation calculation

Aggregated from §9.2. Returns -100 immediately if the pastor is blocked (§11.4).

#### 15.3 Church score calculation

`church_score` is the church-level `activity_score` (§4.1), filtered by reputation
(§4.3/§10.1).
