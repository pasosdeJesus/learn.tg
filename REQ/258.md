## 1. Objective

Give a learner a **simple, offline-first book of accounts** for a small business, good
enough to teach order and honesty with money, and simple enough to be used on a cheap
phone in an area with poor connectivity. Every figure the learner records must be theirs
(private, authenticated) and must survive a lost connection.

The tool is the practice artifact of *Small Business Development: Stewardship &
Purpose* (R-#257): each guide ends with an exercise done in it.

## 2. What exists today

- `app/[lang]/diligent-records/` (page + layout) with its own `public/manifest.json`
  (`Diligent Records`, `start_url: /en/diligent-records`), so it can be installed as a
  separate app; it is linked from the layout and has a `runtimeCaching` entry
  (`/[lang]/diligent-records*`, `diligent-cache`, 30 days) that **must be kept**.
- The page shows a session check (SIWE + wallet), an install prompt and three
  placeholder sections (daily/weekly/monthly) with **no** persistence.
- Offline plumbing to reuse: `lib/offline-db.ts` (`learn-tg-offline` with
  `withStore()`), `lib/offline-guide-db.ts`, `lib/offline-queue-db.ts` +
  `useOfflineQueue` (queue + replay + `lastRejection`), `lib/offline-catalog.ts`.

## 3. Data model (Kysely migration, `db/migrations/`)

- `diligent_account` — the chart of accounts (per user):
  `id`, `usuario_id`, `nombre`, `type` (`asset | liability | income | expense | equity`),
  `currency` (`SLE | USDT | CELO`), `is_active`, timestamps.
  Seeded on first use with a minimal chart (cash, sales, purchases, transport, rent,
  personal withdrawal, savings).
- `diligent_entry` — one movement:
  `id`, `usuario_id`, `date`, `account_id`, `amount` (numeric, signed: positive in,
  negative out), `counter_account_id` (nullable: enables double-entry later without a
  second table), `category`, `note` (max 200 chars, no personal names), `source`
  (`online | offline`), `client_id` (unique per user: the idempotency key of a replayed
  offline entry), `created_at`.
- `diligent_export` — history of exports (`id`, `usuario_id`, `from`, `to`, `created_at`)
  so the user can see what they already downloaded.
- **Derived, never stored**: daily/weekly/monthly summaries and the P&L/balance reports
  are computed from `diligent_entry` (the cache-trigger policy of ARCHITECTURE.md does
  not apply: there is no aggregate table yet; if one is added it must be trigger-fed).

## 4. Design

1. **Offline first.** The tool reads its accounts from IndexedDB (`diligent` store in
   `learn-tg-offline`) and writes entries there first; a queue (same shape as
   `offline-queue-db`, or the existing one) replays them when the connection returns,
   with `client_id` for idempotency. The UI never blocks on the network.
2. **Server routes** (all `authenticateUser` + `doc/api-security.md`): `GET/POST
   /api/diligent/accounts`, `GET/POST /api/diligent/entries` (paged, `from`/`to`),
   `GET /api/diligent/summary`, `GET /api/diligent/export.csv`. `POST` accepts a batch
   of queued entries and answers per item (accepted / duplicate / rejected with reason).
3. **Privacy.** Amounts are the user's business: no third-party `nombre` ever leaves
   the server (the API rule), no PII in logs, and the route list is audited with
   `bin/audit-api-auth.mjs`. Notes are free text but the UI must discourage names
   ("cliente A", not the person's name).
4. **Money in the learner's terms.** Amounts are entered as text with two decimals and
   stored as `numeric(14,2)`; the currency is per account (Leones and USDT side by side,
   no conversion in the tool: converting is the job of `stable-sl`). No crypto amounts
   are moved by the tool: it only records them.
5. **Reports, kept teachable.** Daily, weekly and monthly summaries by category; a
   simple P&L and a cash balance. No jargon: "what came in", "what went out", "what is
   left", and one line per figure explaining what it means (this is course material).
6. **Export.** CSV of the entries of a period, downloadable, so a learner can hand it to
   their church or a microfinance officer. The export is generated server-side and the
   history is recorded in `diligent_export`.
7. **The tool is the course's practice.** Each exercise of R-#257 maps to a concrete
   action in the tool (record a sale, record a purchase, close the week, look at the
   balance). The "demo" placeholders are removed and the page keeps: the install
   prompt, the session/wallet state and the offline indicator.
8. **Its own install and cache.** `public/manifest.json` and the
   `/[lang]/diligent-records*` cache entry stay as they are; the page must work offline
   for the entries already opened (its own `NetworkFirst` page entry plus the
   IndexedDB store).

## 5. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An authenticated user can create accounts, record entries and see daily/weekly/
      monthly summaries; everything is private to that user (another wallet cannot read
      it, including with a valid session of its own).
- [ ] Recording an entry offline works; on reconnect it is replayed exactly once
      (`client_id`), and the summary reflects it.
- [ ] The summary and the P&L/balance are computed from the entries (no stored totals to
      drift) and are explained in plain language for the course.
- [ ] Export to CSV of a period works and is recorded in `diligent_export`.
- [ ] The page still installs as an app and shows the offline indicator; its manifest and
      cache entry are unchanged.
- [ ] `GET /api/diligent/*` and `POST /api/diligent/*` authenticate; `node
      bin/audit-api-auth.mjs` reports **0 failed**.
- [ ] Unit tests for the store, the summary maths and the queue replay; E2E: record an
      entry offline, reconnect, see it in the summary.
- [ ] `make type` and the full suite green.

## 6. Out of scope

- Payroll, taxes, invoicing with legal validity, multi-company or accountant roles.
- Double-entry enforcement, bank reconciliation, imports from bank statements.
- Moving or converting real money (that is `stable-sl` and the payment flows).
- A public/shared view of the accounts (a later "show it to my church" feature, if the
  course asks for it).
